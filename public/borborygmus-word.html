BORBORYGMUS

/ˌbɔːrbəˈrɪɡməs/  ·  bor-buh-RIG-muhs  ·  noun

The Definition

Borborygmus — intestinal rumbling caused by the movement of gas through the intestines.

The rare medical term that doubles as a sound effect. Say it out loud; your stomach already knows it.

Anatomy of the Word

Tap each fragment:

borborygm-
From New Latin, after Greek borborygmos, from the verb borboryzein — "to rumble." The Greeks coined it to imitate the very sound it describes: onomatopoeia wearing a lab coat.
-us
The Latin masculine noun ending — the white coat the word wears in medical texts. Plural: borborygmi (bor-buh-RIG-my).

So: a Greek stomach-noise, dressed in Latin, admitted to English around 1724.

A Rumble in Four Acts

Antiquity — borboryzein

Greek physicians need a verb for the gut's growling, so they mint one that sounds like it: borboryzein, to rumble. The noun borborygmos follows. Few medical terms are this honest about their origins — most hide behind Latin; this one gurgles.

New Latin — the scholars adopt it

Renaissance and early-modern physicians, writing medicine in Latin, lift the Greek noun wholesale and Latinise it: borborygmus. It enters the learned vocabulary as the proper clinical name for a thoroughly undignified noise.

c. 1724 — first known English use

The word crosses into English (Merriam-Webster dates its first known use to about 1724). Polite society gains a dignified way to discuss what happens after beans.

Today — plural borborygmi

Still the clinical term, still faintly ridiculous to say with a straight face. The adjective borborygmic exists for those who need it. Competitive eaters and gastroenterologists remain its primary users.

The Word That Admits It

Most etymologies are archaeology — dead roots, borrowed bones. Borborygmus is rarer: a word whose origin story is its meaning. The Greeks heard the stomach rumble and wrote down the rumble. Three thousand years later, the spelling still gurgles.

"borborygmus — intestinal rumbling caused by moving gas: New Latin, from Greek borborygmos, from borboryzein to rumble." — Merriam-Webster
