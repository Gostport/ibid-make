MYSTICETE

/ˈmɪstɪˌsiːt/  ·  MISS-tih-seet  ·  noun · zoology

The Definition, Translated

Mysticete — a baleen whale. Any whale of the suborder Mysticeti: blue whales, humpbacks, finbacks, right whales. No teeth — rows of baleen plates sieve plankton from the water.

Say it out loud: MISS-tih-seet. Tonight's board hid the largest animal that ever lived behind an ordinary-looking word — and the word itself is a 2,000-year-old typo. This one was a pangram on the board, using all seven letters, and it went unfound. The best word of the night was the one nobody played.

From New Latin mysticētus, from Greek mustikētos — a copyist's error for ho mūs to kētos, "the mouse, the whale."

Anatomy of the Word

One whale, one mouse, one slip of the pen — tap each:

mūs
"The mouse." — Greek mūs, mouse. Aristotle wrote of ho mūs to kētos, "the mouse, the whale" — his phrase for an unknown kind of aquatic animal. Maybe irony, maybe scale: next to the great whales, anything looks like a mouse.
kētos
"The whale." — Greek kētos, sea monster, whale. The root of cetacean, of Cetus the constellation, of every whale-word since.
mustikētos
"The typo." — Some early modern copy of Aristotle's Historia Animalium fused "mouse-whale" into a single word, mustikētos — and the error became the name. The largest animal that ever lived sails under a copyist's slip.

Mouse in the phrase, whale in the sea, typo in the dictionary. Same word, three lives.

What It Is

Three facts that have to be held together:

I. The Baleen Whale

No teeth. Rows of baleen plates hang from the upper jaw and strain plankton from the water like a sieve. The blue whale — the biggest animal known to have ever existed — is a mysticete.

II. The Mouse-Whale

Aristotle called some unknown sea creature "the mouse, the whale." A copyist's slip fused the phrase into one word, and the slip became official. Science kept the typo.

III. The Mustache It Should Have Had

The corrected name is Mystacoceti: mystax (mustache) + ketos (whale). The frayed edge of the baleen looks like a mustache. The better name lost; the typo won.

A Short History of a Typo

Aristotle — The Mouse, the Whale

In Historia Animalium, Aristotle writes of ho mūs to kētos — "the mouse, the whale" — an unknown aquatic animal. Two words, one mystery.

The Copyist — The Slip

Early modern editions miscopy the phrase as a single word, mustikētos. Nobody catches it. The error enters the dictionaries and stays.

1825–35 — The Name Enters English

Mysticete is first recorded in English, and the suborder Mysticeti is named for the typo. The whale gets its title from a transcription error.

Today — Still the Typo

Blue whales, humpbacks, finbacks, right whales — the largest animals ever known, all sailing under a copyist's error. The mustache name, Mystacoceti, waits in the wings, more appropriate and unused.
